  <p class="research-lead">
    Two-particle correlation functions encode information about the space-time structure of the emitting source in
    nuclear reactions. Correlation function imaging aims to reconstruct source information directly from measured
    correlations, turning femtoscopic observables into a more visual and model-aware probe of reaction dynamics.
  </p>

  <div class="research-pill-row">
    <span>Femtoscopy</span>
    <span>Two-particle correlations</span>
    <span>Source imaging</span>
    <span>Inverse problem</span>
  </div>

  <h2>Scientific Questions</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>What source structures are encoded in measured two-particle correlation functions?</li>
    <li>How can the imaging inversion be regularized and validated against model calculations?</li>
    <li>Which features of the reconstructed source are stable under statistical and systematic uncertainty?</li>
  </ul>

  <div class="research-two-column">
